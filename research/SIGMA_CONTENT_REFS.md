### Tone

No AI slop.

- No em dashes.
- No "not X, but Y". Say the thing, then say the next thing in its own sentence.
- No filler. If a sentence can go, it goes.
- No brochure words. Harry Dry's list of words real people skip is the right ban list: unlock, unleash, enhance, exceed, empower, supercharge ([17 tips](https://marketingexamples.com/copywriting/tips)).
- One idea on a frame. Chris Do says his engagement moved when he cut carousels to one idea a slide, and that a slide with two paragraphs is where people stop ([The Futur](https://thefutur.com/watch/instagram-carousel-design-content-marketing-advice)).
- The first sentence carries the fact. Paul Graham, reading YC applications at about 100 a day: put it in the first sentence, in the simplest words ([How to Apply](https://www.ycombinator.com/howtoapply/)).
- Specifics you can check. "You can't bullshit specifics" is Harry Dry's line, and it matches the Dossair pages ([7 practical ways](https://marketingexamples.com/copywriting/conversion)).
- A point of view someone could argue with, tied to a file you have. Wes Kao's test: if everyone already agrees, it is the middle of the road ([Spiky point of view](https://www.weskao.com/blog/spiky-point-of-view-lets-get-a-little-controversial)).

House voice is already on the site. Short sentences. A date. A count. What broke, written down. The blog does this for a month of the product ([91 programmes](https://rizqey.com/blog/dossair)). New slides should sound like that page, with the stale lines taken out (see the number box below).

### Same PNGs, three different posts

Instagram. Up to 20 photos and videos in one carousel. One orientation for the whole post. The feed shows slide 1 and a row of dots. The grid shows slide 1 only. Likes and comments sit on the whole post. A single slide has no like count of its own ([Instagram Help](https://help.instagram.com/269314186824048)). One tag and one hashtag fits even the strict reading.

LinkedIn. Organic carousels were removed on 14 Dec 2023. Old carousel posts came down. Carousel ads were left in place ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a764804)). What people still call a carousel is a document post: PDF, PPT, PPTX, DOC, or DOCX, up to 100 MB and 300 pages. Give it a title. Members can download it as a PDF ([documents](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a518909)). A multi-image post allows up to 20 photos. The first image gets the layout. Tall sets are held to 4:5 and the bottom row can crop ([photos](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a527229)). For these PNGs, upload them as one PDF in a shared order. A vendor guide says 6 to 12 slides is the practical band, against the 300-page ceiling ([Decktopus](https://www.decktopus.com/blog/linkedin-carousel-size-dimensions)). That 6 to 12 is vendor advice. LinkedIn's own ceiling is 300 pages.

X. There is no organic swipe carousel of 8 to 20 frames. PostDecks, last checked 17 Oct 2026, measured the iPhone app on 3 Oct 2026: up to 4 photos, two side by side, three or four in a strip you swipe ([PostDecks](https://postdecks.com/multiple-photos-per-post)). The X help page for photos returned 403 on this pass, so the official help text was not read. X carousel ads are a different product: Sprout lists 2 to 6 images for ads ([Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/social-media-image-sizes-guide/)). Do not plan an organic post as an ad unit. Velocity's publishing note matches the four-image organic limit ([Velocity](https://www.velocity.li/blog/best-tools-publish-carousel-posts-instagram-linkedin-x-2026)).

Cut for 23 Oct. This is a recommendation from those limits. No study produced it.

- A rules post or a points table: 6 frames. X uses the first 4, and frames 5 and 6 are the second post, with frame 5 able to stand alone.
- A proof story: 8 frames. Same split, 4 and 4.
- An event logistics post: 7 frames. First 4 are the date, the city, the seats, and the close. The last 3 are the weekend, for Instagram and the LinkedIn PDF.

Harry Dry's 30-slide handbook worked as a handbook (see A1). It is the wrong length for a phone swipe in 2026. Chris Do's "one idea, then stop" is the length rule. A 5-point idea is a hook, the points, and a close. Padding to 10 or 20 throws away the taste.

## Track A. Dossair

Dossair tracks accelerators, grants, and hackathons, drafts each answer from one page of facts, checks the fact, the length, and the voice, fills the real form, and stops before submit. It never types a password. The person presses submit. It runs on RL Tokens, plans at $20, $50, $100, or $200 a month, or $999 on a call. On 22 Oct 2026 the homepage says it runs on Faris's own applications, and other founders are next ([homepage](https://dossair.rizqey.com/)).

The interesting public fact is the opposite of a magic-apply ad. On 23 Sep 2026 a bot was told to submit. ### Hooks that held up

1. A count with the split under it. "30 slides." "$52,843" and then four products. "26 fields. 24 filled. 2 only mine."
2. The form's own question as the first line. YC's first look is "What is your company going to make?" Dossair's sample screen uses "What are you building, and for whom?"
3. One sentence a stranger could rebuild. Graham's test: if the reader is no closer to picturing the thing, the sentence was empty. His bad example is "We are going to transform the relationship between individuals and information." His usable example is a database with a wiki-like interface.
4. A before and after of one line, with the vague line left on the slide so the specific line can be seen. Harry Dry's landing-page rewrites.
5. A flaw, dated. The blog's "sent, and still marked as a draft for 23 days" is stronger than a feature list.
6. A debatable line you can defend with the file. "The draft is the easy part. The form is the work." Wes Kao's bar: someone could disagree, and you still have the 23 Sep note.

Copy: the 48-hour hold on the same frame as the shop, and a one-line disclosure in the caption of any post that earns points ("I post for SigmaHack KL points"). Leave alone: beehiiv's cash commissions and tiers, unless SigmaHack adds them on the page. The spendable list on 22 Oct 2026 is merch, AI credits, and the attendee pass.

**C11. Steam Points Shop**
[Official page](https://store.steampowered.com/points/howitworks/). The fetch did not return the rules text, only the store chrome, so the official wording was not read. Unverified from the primary page. A PC Gamer piece on the permanent shop says points accrue at 100 per US dollar spent, adjusted for currency, that they buy cosmetics and community awards, that they do not expire, and that they cannot be traded ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/steam-launches-a-permanent-points-shop/)). A Steam discussion quotes the refund rule: refunding the purchase removes the points, and a negative balance removes the newest items bought with points ([discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/discussions/forum/1/3081006600720610598/)). The rate and the refund path are secondary sources until the official page is read in a browser.

Taste: a shop with a permanent catalog, and a clawback if the underlying spend is undone. The clawback is the part worth copying. People accept a shop when the reversal rule is boring and public.

Copy: "points can be reversed" deserves one frame of its own, with two examples that are true. A bought like. A post that drops the tag. If there are more reversal cases, they should be written on the points page before they are written on a slide. Leave alone: the 100-points-per-dollar rate, and any cosmetic-shop language. Unverified here, and it is a different economy. SigmaHack points are earned by posting.

**C12. Marc Lou, TrustMRR**
[Newsletter, 15 Nov 2025](https://newsletter.marclou.com/p/i-made-an-app-in-24-hours-and-20-378-the-next-day). He says he saw a Pieter Levels post about fake MRR screenshots, built a page where founders connect a read-only Stripe key, launched by quoting that post, and sold sidebar ads from $299 up to $1,499 until the side project had made $20,378 in about three days. He says the launch tweet passed 3,000 likes and 2 million views. This is his account. Stripe was not checked. The live tweet was not opened. The page he describes had one headline, two buttons, and a leaderboard.

Taste: the leaderboard is the product, and it is tied to a source the founder does not type in by hand. One headline. The story has a cause (the fake screenshots) so the table means something.

Copy: the SigmaHack board already refuses hand-waved points: the tag and the hashtag are how a post is counted, and bought engagement is out. A post can show an empty board and the rule, which is the honest version of his "8 startups on day one." Leave alone: his dollar figures, the price ladder, and a claim that a SigmaHack post will do 2 million views. Also leave alone building a public board of anyone's revenue. That is a different product.

**C13. The Futur, again, for the table post**
Same video as A14, [The Futur](https://thefutur.com/watch/instagram-carousel-design-content-marketing-advice). Used here for a different reason. A points post fails his "light but satisfying" test the moment two tables share a frame. He got tired of a carousel that put paragraphs on a slide. A 6-row engage table plus a 3-row post table on one 1080x1350 is that failure.

Taste: the critique is visual, and it is the right edit for this track. The content is a table. The craft is not making the table smaller until it is a footnote.

Copy: one table a frame, type large enough to read on a phone without zooming. The engage table can be two frames if 6 rows feel tight: the single actions, then the bundles and the cap. Leave alone: a "save this" frame that repeats the table in 11-point type. Frame 6 is the pass and the builder seat. The save happens because the numbers were readable.

**C14. Harry Dry, "Let your customers write your copy"**
[Essay](https://marketingexamples.com/copywriting/customers). Design Academy asks new people "what is your biggest frustration with design?" and writes from the sheet of answers. Laura's line, which he quotes: they did not write the line, the customers did, and it kept coming back.

Taste: the words already exist. For a points programme the words show up in the first fifty comments. "Does a quote count if I also repost?" is a better frame than any headline written in advance.

Copy: a follow-up carousel made only of real questions, one per frame, answered with the row from the table. Ship it after the rules post, and only with questions people actually asked. Leave alone: invented FAQs. The Sea x OpenAI page is what a made-up FAQ pile looks like when the answers then contradict each other (B12).

**C15. Visualize Value, again, for a single number**
Same practice as A13, [about](https://visualizevalue.com/about). One number can be the whole frame: 55, 150, 1,000, 48. The practice is the permission to leave the frame almost empty.

Taste: a points system gets cheap the moment every frame is a gradient, a coin icon, and a badge. One number in one weight, on the same cream or white as the rest of the set, looks like the rules page. That match is the brand.

Copy: frame 1 as "55" with "one video" under it, for as long as the board is empty. Then retire it. Leave alone: a coin, a flame, a streak, a gold laurel. Strava's laurel means something because it is tied to a hill and 90 days (C7). A laurel on a SigmaHack frame would be a sticker.

### Copy, and what to leave alone, for the whole track

Copy the table, the cap, the hold, the two piles, the pass, the reversal, and the empty board while it is empty.

Leave alone: bought-engagement tactics, pods, "comment YES", and any reward for a comment under 5 words. The page already sets the floor. Leave alone a promise that X points spend. They count for rank. Leave alone double points for likes. Accepted builders double on posts. Leave alone Duolingo's guilt streak and Steam's unpaid balance. And take the rules post down, or fix frame 1, the hour the board is no longer empty.
